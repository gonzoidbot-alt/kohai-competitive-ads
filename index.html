Winning patterns — what the category has converged on

Video is the default weapon. 20 of 27 actives across the three live SMB buyers are video-first. Only Marblism tested a carousel.

Pain-first hooks about being the bottleneck. The winning move is naming the 11pm-and-still-working moment, not naming the product.

Sintra: "You're the single point of failure in your own business" · Lindy: "…responding to clients at 2am?" · Marblism: "Hand over the endless emails, lead gen, and daily chaos"

Personification sells. Marblism names every AI employee (Eva, Sonny, Stan, Rachel, Penny) and gives each a task; Sintra sells "12 AI helpers" as a team. Abstract "AI platform" copy is rare.

"It's like having 12 colleagues" — Sintra testimonial

Category reframes vs ChatGPT. Position against the generic chatbot, not against other AI-employee tools.

Sintra: "ChatGPT is for hobbies. Sintra is for business." · Lindy: "It's literally ChatGPT but made specifically for agency owners."

Anti-fear copy matters. Marblism runs reassurance angles repeatedly: "AI is not replacing humans, it's replacing tasks."

Offers are bundle/quantity-led, not feature-led.

Sintra: "Pay for 1, get 12", up to 70% off · Marblism: "Stop paying $4,000 and waiting months for a basic website"

CTA splits by motion. Self-serve product (Sintra, Marblism, Grok) drives Sign-up; demo/proof motions (Lindy, OpenAI, Relevance AI) drive Learn-more.

◆ NEW ENTRANT — Town (town.com), added Oct 19

Town just raised a $108M Series B at a $1B valuation today (Oct 19) — a16z-led Series A in June, out of beta. Their paid program started Sep 30, 2026: ~180 archived ads, ~24 active cards, all born Sep 30 → Oct 17. Two creative families only, heavily versioned, 100% Sign-up → town.com.

  • The attention-hierarchy reframe — 'Before I check Slack, Gmail, or Calendar, Town tells me what matters.' They attack the tools, not ChatGPT, not other AI assistants. A categorically different wedge from everyone else in this set.
  • Calm-competence positioning — 'working quietly in the background', 'handles the busywork', 'effortlessly'. No panic, no bottleneck-fear. Contrast with Lindy/Sintra's 2am-agency-owner hooks.
  • Message discipline — 2 creatives, ~24 placements, versions of one belief. They're testing economics before scaling spend (post-Series-B, expect that to change).
  • Lane overlap with Kohai: low today. Town sells 'an extension of you' (executive/prosumer); the SMB field sells 'a team that works for you'. If Town pivots to teams post-B, they collide with Kohai at $108M of fresh fuel.

⚠ GROK PIVOT — verified Oct 17

The biggest spender in the category adopted the SMB playbook two weeks after launch. The Sep 28 engine-led burst ('Grok 4.7 is our most intelligent model…') is gone from actives entirely — zero model-led creative remains live. The current set is 100% creator-partnership ads:

  • Named AI employees at xAI scale — 'Scout for research, Milo for the office, and Otis for my LinkedIn' — Marblism's personification pattern, now backed by the largest budget in the category
  • Category language — 'AI employees to perform specific tasks' — the SMB field's own positioning, adopted wholesale
  • Pain hooks — 'admin that used to eat my mornings' from the founder of a $2M design studio
  • Comment-gated CTAs — 'Comment "Grok" and I'll DM you the link' on 9 of 11 actives, a funnel pattern no SMB competitor runs
  • What they still don't do: verticals. No construction, trades, or site-work language anywhere in the live set — the construction campaign lane stays fully open

Strategic read: the moat is vertical depth + speed, not the angle. Assume any lane Kohai opens is contestable within a quarter.

Gaps for Kohai — observed openings, not invented ones

Solo operators are unclaimed

Lindy's hook names agency owners; Sintra/Marblism speak to generic "business owners." A solo-operator-specific hook family is unclaimed in the current active set.

Pricing transparency is absent

No active ad leads with self-serve pricing; offers are vague ("Get early access", "free to try"). A price-led static could stand out immediately.

Static image is underused

Only 2 of 27 SMB actives are statics. Cheap statics against the video-heavy field is a cheap in-feed differentiator.

Single-role wedges are open

No evergreen "AI employee for [specific job]" campaigns except Marblism's per-character ads — "AI receptionist" / "AI bookkeeper" wedges are open.

Enterprise proof is unclaimed

Relevance AI vacated the Meta channel. Their Mar 2026 pattern (named customers, hard numbers) has zero active competition in category placements.

The giants don't do pain

Grok sells the engine, OpenAI sells stories. Neither runs bottleneck-relief hooks — the category's proven emotional lever is open against the biggest spenders.
